## Activity 1 — Tinkercad name keychains

### Resources used

- 20 individual Windows computers
- Tinkercad Classroom
- 9 Bambu Lab X1 Carbon printers
- PLA filament
- USB flash drive
- ball-bead chains

### Procedure

1. Do not assume students know Windows.
2. Demonstrate the Start menu, Chrome, the address bar, and entering `tinkercad.com`.
3. Have students join the Tinkercad Classroom using the code on their name tags.
4. Demonstrate basic shapes, resizing, moving, text, and holes.
5. Have students create their own flat keychain rather than modify a template.
6. Require a built-in hole and raised name.
7. Explain that all geometry must connect because printing occurs layer by layer.
8. Circulate and help students individually.
9. Export each design as STL.
10. Collect files from every computer after students leave.
11. Repair floating geometry using Tinkercad Classroom.
12. Slice and print for distribution at the next session.
13. Attach ball-bead chains.

### Actual printing experience

- Individual keychain: approximately 30 minutes
- About four keychains per printer
- Multiple color changes: approximately two hours per printer
- Students selected from about seven PLA colors
- Parents and students loved the finished keychains

### Recommended change

Use one base color and one contrasting text color. Preserve shape creativity while simplifying slicing and printing.

## Activity 2 — Paper LED circuits

### Supplies per student

- cardstock template
- CR2032 battery
- one LED for the robot template or two for fireflies
- at least 12 inches of copper tape, plus extra
- scissors
- colored pencils

### Procedure

1. Use one template for the entire class.
2. Show LED polarity, battery orientation, tape paths, and the paper switch.
3. Build one complete sample before distributing materials.
4. Have students place the tape in long, clean sections.
5. Test the circuit.
6. Troubleshoot gaps, poor overlaps, reversed LEDs, and battery orientation.
7. Have students decorate the front.
8. Students take the project home.

### What went wrong

- Three different circuit designs were taught at once.
- The adhesive side of the tape conducted poorly.
- Students cut tape too short and patched gaps unsuccessfully.
- There were not enough scissors.

### Recommendation

Use the one-LED robot template as the standard lesson. Keep the two-LED firefly template as an optional extension.

## Activity 3 — Paper-airplane weight distribution

### Supplies

- one sheet of 8.5 × 11-inch paper per student
- one identical paper clip per student
- data table
- pencil
- blue painter’s tape
- three 25-foot tape measures

### Procedure

1. Demonstrate one consistent arrowhead-style airplane using an overhead view.
2. Have students write their names on the planes.
3. Teach students how to read the tape measure.
4. Ask for a prediction.
5. Test four conditions: no clip, front, middle, and back.
6. Line students shoulder to shoulder.
7. Have everyone throw simultaneously.
8. Keep everyone behind the line until all planes land.
9. Retrieve, measure, and record.
10. Compare results with predictions.

### Actual experience

Approximately seven students could not initially read a tape measure. Plan to teach this explicitly.
