## Vibrating art robots

### Purchased specifically

For 20 students:

- 20 switched two-AA battery holders
- 24 Type 130 DC motors, allowing four spares
- heat-shrink tubing

### Check inventory or source locally

- 20 standard 18-ounce red plastic party cups
- 40 AA batteries
- painter’s tape
- 20 jumbo hot-glue sticks
- approximately 60 markers
- approximately 40 rubber bands
- soldering tools
- helping hands
- large kraft-paper test surface

### Friday preparation

1. Pair each motor with a switched battery holder.
2. Solder the leads.
3. Cover connections with heat-shrink tubing.
4. Test every assembly.
5. Make spares.
6. Use an extra motor shaft to make three mounting holes in each jumbo glue stick: left, center, and right.
7. Create an assembly-line workflow.

Students should not need to solder.

### Student assembly

1. Turn the cup upside down.
2. Tape the motor and battery holder to the top.
3. Insert two AA batteries.
4. Mount the glue-stick counterweight on the motor shaft.
5. Choose three marker colors.
6. Hold three markers around the cup with two rubber bands.
7. Adjust all tips until the robot stands evenly.
8. Turn on the built-in switch.
9. Test on kraft paper.
10. Move the glue stick among its three holes and describe changes in the student’s own words.

### Troubleshooting

**Robot falls over:** reposition the markers until they are evenly spaced and all tips contact the paper.

**Robot barely moves:** check battery charge, motor operation, counterweight fit, and whether the marker tips create too much drag.

**Assembly shifts:** add painter’s tape.

Painter’s tape was used instead of hot glue because it was easy, adjustable, and avoided heat damage to thin plastic cups.

### Safety

The small motor could be stopped by hand and did not present a significant hazard in the original activity. Still keep hair, loose clothing, and faces away from the rotating counterweight.

### Take-home

Students took the art bots home.

## Makey Makey instruments

### Resources

- 12 Makey Makey kits already owned by the classroom
- computers with Scratch
- copper tape
- paper
- colored pencils
- jumper/alligator leads included with the kits

### Grouping

Students worked in pairs. With 20 students and 12 kits, ten kits were used and two remained available as spares.

### Procedure

1. Pair students.
2. Connect each Makey Makey to a computer.
3. Open Scratch.
4. Demonstrate a working instrument.
5. Noel’s example was a piano.
6. Have pairs draw and color an instrument on paper.
7. Add copper-tape touch points.
8. Connect the Makey Makey inputs.
9. Program Scratch to play sounds.
10. Test and revise.

Some students copied the piano example; others created guitars or other instruments.

### Experience

The activity went very well. Its primary limitation is equipment access: an organizer needs Makey Makeys or must substitute another activity.
