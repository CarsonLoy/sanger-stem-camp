# Sanger STEM Camp Playbook — Project Brief

## Working name

**Sanger STEM Camp Playbook**

Recommended public title:

> Sanger STEM Camp Playbook  
> A complete, practical guide to hosting your own three-day STEM camp.

Recommended domain:

`SangerSTEMCamp.org`

The original event should remain named **Sanger STEM Camp**. The word **Playbook** tells visitors that the website is a reproducible how-to guide rather than only an event website.

## Purpose

Build a public, free, step-by-step website that allows a teacher, high-school team, robotics team, library, makerspace, parent group, or community organization to host the same camp or adapt it to their available resources.

The site must answer:

- What must be purchased?
- What might already exist in a classroom?
- When should each item be purchased?
- What must be prepared before each Saturday?
- How should the room be arranged?
- What should presenters demonstrate?
- What should helpers do?
- What common failures should organizers expect?
- What should happen during breaks, food service, pickup, and cleanup?

The main experience must be chronological and checklist-driven.

## Guiding principle

> An organizer should be able to keep the website open while planning and running the camp, proceed from top to bottom, and rarely need to ask, “What do I do next?”

## Audience

Primary:

- High-school STEM and robotics teams
- Teachers and engineering pathways
- Libraries and makerspaces
- Community organizations
- College outreach groups

Secondary:

- Parents
- Scout groups
- Youth programs
- Schools without dedicated STEM staff

## Content priorities

Use:

- Checklists
- Numbered procedures
- Timelines
- Exact or recommended quantities
- Short troubleshooting trees
- Downloadable templates
- Photos that clarify construction or setup
- Clearly labeled estimates and TODOs

Avoid:

- Long narrative introductions
- Scattering one activity across many pages
- Treating the site mainly as promotion
- Assuming every organizer owns the same equipment
- Inventing missing facts

## Required distinction

Every item and resource must be classified as one of the following:

1. **Buy for this camp** — purchased specifically for the Sanger STEM Camp.
2. **Check your inventory** — common classroom or makerspace materials that Sanger West already owned.
3. **Specialized equipment** — computers, printers, Makey Makeys, soldering tools, projectors, and similar infrastructure.
4. **Source locally or collect** — ordinary materials such as cardboard, straws, cups, and two-liter bottles.
5. **Optional upgrade** — improves the experience but is not required.

The website should show both:

- the **incremental cost** paid by Sanger STEM Camp; and
- the **from-scratch cost** for an organizer who owns nothing.

## Teaching approach

- Do not leave a student behind.
- Begin with probing questions and hints.
- Increase direct assistance when needed.
- Ensure every child completes or meaningfully participates in the activity.
- Do not assume elementary students know Windows, browsers, tape measures, or makerspace tools.

## Weekly workflow used

- Monday–Tuesday: decide activities and work out the format
- Wednesday: order missing supplies
- Friday: print, prepare, solder, test, organize, and set up
- Saturday: run camp, clean up, and note improvements

This was the actual workflow, not necessarily the recommended planning lead time.
